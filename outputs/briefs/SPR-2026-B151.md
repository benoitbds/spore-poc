## Abstract

If a high-fidelity, multi-physics digital twin of a twin-roll casting (TRC) process is integrated with an Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF) for data assimilation from in-line sensors, then the EnKF will estimate internal solidification state variables (e.g., mushy zone thickness, cooling rate) with a root-mean-square error (RMSE) ≤15% relative to direct experimental measurements, because the EnKF's capacity to handle non-linear, transient dynamics and assimilate noisy sensor data—proven in liquid rocket engine control—can be transferred to model the coupled thermal-fluid-mechanical fields governing rapid solidification.

### 1.3 Chaine causale

1. Step 1: An EnKF assimilates real-time sensor data (e.g., roll surface temperature, roll separating force) into a high-fidelity, transient TRC process model (coupled CFD-thermodynamics), reducing uncertainty in the estimated internal thermal and solidification fields.
1. Step 2: The improved state estimation (e.g., of temperature gradient G and solidification rate R) allows for the accurate prediction of local cooling rates (≈ G*R) and undercooling within the roll gap.
1. Step 3: The accurately predicted local solidification conditions determine the nucleation density and growth kinetics, which govern the final as-cast grain size and morphology according to solidification theory.

**Hypotheses cles :**

- The core multi-physics phenomena in TRC (fluid flow, heat transfer, solidification, mechanical deformation) can be modeled with sufficient fidelity to capture first-order effects on microstructure.
- The EnKF algorithm is robust to moderate structural model errors (e.g., in the interfacial heat transfer coefficient) and sensor noise characteristics typical of industrial TRC lines.
- Key internal state variables (e.g., mushy zone profile) are experimentally accessible via a reference method (e.g., rapid quench interruption) for validation.

**Inconnues identifiees :**

- The exact bandwidth and phase lag of the primary TRC actuators (roll gap servo, cooling valve) under dynamic operating conditions.
- The proportion of final grain size variance attributable to macroscale process parameters versus stochastic nucleation events.
- The optimal covariance inflation parameters for the EnKF to maintain filter stability against unmodeled TRC dynamics.

### 1.4 Conditions aux limites

- **Process operates within the 'stable casting window' defined by the alloy's hot tearing susceptibility and roll sticking limits.** — Outside this window, defect formation dominates microstructure, invalidating the solidification kinetics model.
- **Sensor data assimilation cycle time is ≤100 ms.** — The dominant thermal dynamics in the roll gap have time constants on the order of 100-500 ms; slower updates fail to track state evolution.
- **Alloy composition is within the range of commercial AZ31 or a similar Mg/Al alloy.** — The solidification kinetics and phase diagrams for these alloys are well-characterized, providing a stable basis for the physics-based model component.

### 1.5 Cadre theorique

Control-Theoretic State Estimation and Data Assimilation, applied to Rapid Solidification Processing.

## 4. Protocole experimental

**Timeline globale**: 8-14 months
**Budget global**: €25k-120k

### 4.1 Phase 1 — In Silico Validation

**Objectif**: To determine if the EnKF algorithm, when coupled to a simplified but representative TRC model, can achieve state estimation errors below 15% under simulated sensor noise and model errors, and to assess sensitivity to key unknown parameters.

**Methodologie**: 1. Develop a 2D axisymmetric or 2.5D reduced-order model of TRC in COMSOL Multiphysics or OpenFOAM, capturing coupled heat transfer, fluid flow (VOF), and solidification (enthalpy-porosity). Use material properties for AZ31. 2. Implement an Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF) in Python (using libraries like `pyDA` or `filterpy`) to assimilate synthetic sensor data (roll surface temperature at 3-5 axial positions, roll separating force) generated by the model with added Gaussian noise (5-10% amplitude). 3. Run twin experiments: a 'truth' simulation and an 'estimation' simulation where the EnKF assimilates noisy data into a model with intentional errors (e.g., ±30% error in interfacial heat transfer coefficient, IHTC). 4. Perform Monte Carlo sensitivity analysis: vary EnKF parameters (ensemble size, covariance inflation), IHTC, and sensor noise characteristics. Quantify RMSE of mushy zone thickness and cooling rate against the 'truth' simulation.

- Cout: €0-2,000 (primarily software licenses if not available; OpenFOAM is open-source)
- Duree: 4-8 weeks
- Equipement: High-performance workstation (32+ GB RAM, multi-core CPU)
- Logiciels: COMSOL Multiphysics with CFD & Heat Transfer modules (or OpenFOAM), Python 3.x with NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, pandas, pyDA/filterpy, Git for version control

**Criteres de succes :**

- RMSE of estimated mushy zone thickness: ≤ 15% (relative to simulated 'truth') for ≥80% of the tested operational points (casting speed, roll gap).
- Sensitivity to IHTC error: A ±30% perturbation in the model's IHTC causes a < ±25% change in the EnKF-estimated average cooling rate.
- Filter stability: EnKF does not diverge (error remains bounded) over a simulated casting duration of 60 seconds for all tested tuning parameters within a reasonable range.

- **GO**: Success Criteria 1 AND 2 are met, demonstrating the core estimation concept is viable in silico and robust to a key model error.
- **NO-GO**: Success Criterion 1 is not met (RMSE >15%) even under ideal simulated conditions (low noise, perfect model), indicating a fundamental flaw in the EnKF application or model observability.
- **PIVOT**: Success Criterion 1 is met but Criterion 2 is not (high sensitivity to IHTC). Pivot to Phase 2 focusing on experimental IHTC characterization or to an EnKF variant more robust to parametric errors (e.g., dual estimation).

### 4.2 Phase 2 — Minimal Experimental Validation

**Objectif**: To validate the EnKF's core estimation mechanism using a single, well-instrumented pilot-scale TRC experiment with a quench-interruption, providing one direct comparison between estimated and measured internal state.

**Methodologie**: 1. Select one 'nominal' operating condition within the stable window (e.g., 1.5 m/min, 2.5 mm gap, 30K superheat). 2. Instrument a pilot TRC line with key sensors: infrared pyrometers or embedded thermocouples for roll surface temperature (≥3 locations), load cells for roll separating force. Data acquisition at ≥10 Hz. 3. Conduct a casting run under nominal conditions. Simultaneously, run the EnKF digital twin (developed in Phase 1, now with a refined model) in real-time or post-process, assimilating the live sensor data. 4. At a predetermined steady-state moment, execute an emergency quench interruption (e.g., rapid roll separation and water jet immersion) to freeze the solidification structure in situ. 5. Section the quenched strip along the centerline. Perform metallographic preparation (mounting, polishing, etching). Measure the mushy zone thickness (distance between 0.2 and 0.8 solid fraction boundaries) via quantitative image analysis of the microstructure (e.g., using ImageJ with grain boundary contrast). 6. Compare this single, ground-truth measurement to the EnKF's estimated mushy zone thickness profile at the exact time of quench.

- Cout: €8,000-15,000 (primarily for pilot line time, consumables (alloy), sensor rental/use, and metallography)
- Duree: 6-12 weeks
- Equipement: Pilot-scale Twin-Roll Caster (e.g., laboratory unit), Data Acquisition System (NI cDAQ or similar, ≥10 Hz), Infrared Pyrometers (2-3) or embedded roll thermocouples, Load Cells on roll bearings, Quench system (fast-acting valves, water jets), Metallography lab (cutting, mounting, polishing, etching stations), Optical microscope with digital camera
- Logiciels: Real-time capable EnKF code from Phase 1 (Python/C++), Data acquisition software (LabVIEW, Python DAQmx), Image analysis software (ImageJ, Olympus Stream)

**Criteres de succes :**

- Single-point RMSE: The absolute difference between the EnKF-estimated and the measured mushy zone thickness at the quench moment is ≤15% of the measured value.
- Sensor-to-estimate correlation: The EnKF-estimated roll separating force shows a correlation coefficient R > 0.8 with the measured force during steady-state casting prior to quench.

- **GO**: Success Criterion 1 is met (≤15% error on the quench experiment), providing the first physical evidence supporting the hypothesis.
- **NO-GO**: The error exceeds 30%, indicating a major discrepancy between the model+EnKF and reality that cannot be explained by measurement uncertainty alone.
- **PIVOT**: Error is between 15% and 30%. Pivot to a detailed failure analysis: re-examine model assumptions (e.g., constitutive laws), IHTC calibration from the quench data, or sensor placement/fidelity before considering Phase 3.

### 4.3 Phase 3 — Full Experimental Protocol

**Objectif**: To rigorously validate the hypothesis across the operational window, establish statistical significance, and quantify the explained variance in final grain size.

**Methodologie**: 1. Design of Experiments (DoE): Implement a full or fractional factorial design across the 4 independent variables (casting speed, roll gap, superheat, cooling), with 3-5 center points for a total of ~20-30 casting trials. 2. For each trial: run the EnKF digital twin in real-time for state estimation and record all data. Produce a strip long enough for microstructural characterization (~2-3 meters). 3. Post-process: For each strip, cut samples for metallography. Measure final average grain size using Electron Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD) or automated optical microscopy (linear intercept method, n≥500 grains per sample). 4. Validation: For a subset of trials (e.g., 5-8 spanning the operational space), perform rapid quench interruptions to obtain direct mushy zone thickness measurements for RMSE calculation as in Phase 2. 5. Data Analysis: a) Perform one-sample t-tests to evaluate if the RMSE across quench trials is ≤15%. b) Perform multi-factor ANOVA on the full dataset to determine the proportion of grain size variance (R²) explained by the four macroscale parameters and their interactions. c) Correlate the EnKF-estimated solidification conditions (cooling rate, G*R) with the measured grain size using regression analysis.

- Cout: €40,000-100,000+ (major costs: extensive pilot line time, alloy material for ~30 trials, EBSD analysis time, labor)
- Duree: 6-12 months
- Equipement: Pilot-scale Twin-Roll Caster with full actuator control, Comprehensive sensor suite (as Phase 2, plus possibly more), Quench system for subset of trials, Metallography lab, Scanning Electron Microscope with EBSD capability (or high-end automated optical microscope)
- Logiciels: Robust, real-time EnKF digital twin, Statistical analysis software (JMP, Minitab, or Python statsmodels), EBSD data analysis software (Oxford Instruments AZtec, EDAX OIM)

**Criteres de succes :**

- Prediction 1: RMSE of mushy zone thickness: One-sample t-test confirms RMSE ≤ 15% across validation trials (p < 0.05, one-tailed).
- Prediction 2: Sensitivity to IHTC error: Paired t-test confirms that a ±30% IHTC perturbation causes a < ±25% change in estimated cooling rate (p < 0.05).
- Prediction 3: Explained variance in grain size: ANOVA shows R² ≥ 0.40 for the model containing the four macroscale parameters (p < 0.05 for the overall model).

- **GO**: All three success criteria are met, providing strong, statistically significant evidence supporting the published hypothesis.
- **NO-GO**: Prediction 1 (the core RMSE claim) is statistically rejected (RMSE >15%, p > 0.05), invalidating the main premise of the work.
- **PIVOT**: Predictions 1 and 2 are met, but Prediction 3 is not (R² < 0.40). Pivot the narrative to focus on state estimation accuracy, acknowledging that stochastic effects dominate final grain size, and explore using the EnKF estimates for other purposes (defect prediction).

## 7. Gap Manifest residuel

### 7.1 Data gaps

- Methodological Transfer Gap: None of the provided papers explicitly demonstrate the transfer of control frameworks or ML methods specifically developed for liquid rocket engines (e.g., from DLR) to any materials processing domain. The hypothesis of a direct methodological transfer from aerospace propulsion remains unsupported by the evidence.
- Microstructure Control Gap: While several papers (1eb0b7c..., 4047d524..., c760f2c7...) discuss digital twins for process control, none explicitly demonstrate the active, in-situ control of final microstructure features (e.g., texture, grain size distribution) as the primary control objective. The link from process parameters to microstructure as a controlled variable is not evidenced.
