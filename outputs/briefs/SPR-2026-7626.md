## Abstract

If a catalytic material possesses a reversible surface reconstruction pathway with an activation energy (Ea) between 0.7 and 1.2 eV, and operates under reaction conditions where a key intermediate modulates the surface free energy, then the system will establish a self-sustained limit cycle oscillation in active site density (θ) with an amplitude (Δθ) of 0.05-0.20 ML and a period (τ) of 10-1000 s, because the reaction rate and surface reconstruction are coupled through a negative feedback loop where product/intermediate formation drives reconstruction to a less active state, which subsequently decomposes/desorbs to regenerate the original, more active surface.

### 1.3 Chaine causale

1. Step 1: Catalytic turnover on metallic surface (M^0) produces a key oxygen-containing intermediate or product (e.g., O*, CO*) that incorporates into the subsurface or surface, increasing its chemical potential.
1. Step 2: Subsurface/surface accumulation of this species (e.g., O) induces a thermodynamic driving force for surface reconstruction from a metallic (M^0) to an oxidized (M^δ+O) phase, reducing the density of active sites (θ) by a quantifiable amount Δθ (0.05-0.20 ML).
1. Step 3: The reconstructed (oxidized) phase exhibits lower activity for the initial reactant dissociation (e.g., CH4, H2), causing the concentration of the driving intermediate (O*) to drop via its continued reaction or desorption.
1. Step 4: Depletion of the intermediate reduces the thermodynamic stability of the reconstructed phase, triggering its reversal to the original metallic surface (M^0), thereby restoring the active site density (θ) to its initial value, completing the limit cycle.

**Hypotheses cles :**

- The kinetics of the reversible surface reconstruction (Steps 2 & 4) are faster than the rate of irreversible deactivation pathways (e.g., sintering, coking). Specifically, k_reconstruct > 10 * k_sinter at operating T.
- The reaction intermediate (e.g., O*) that drives reconstruction is part of the main catalytic cycle and its surface coverage is directly coupled to the reaction rate.
- In situ characterization techniques (e.g., ETEM, AP-XPS) can quantify θ and surface phase with sufficient temporal resolution (< τ/10) and without beam-induced artifacts altering the oscillatory state.

**Inconnues identifiees :**

- The quantitative relationship between the amplitude of oscillation (Δθ) and the net long-term stability enhancement (reduction in d<θ>/dt) is unknown.
- Whether the oscillatory state can be maintained across a wide enough 'window' of operating conditions (T, P) to be technologically useful is unknown.
- The minimum nanoparticle size or facet requirement to establish and sustain a coherent oscillatory reconstruction across the particle is unknown.

### 1.4 Conditions aux limites

- **Operating temperature must be below the Tammann temperature of the catalyst material.** — Above the Tammann temperature, bulk diffusion and sintering kinetics become dominant, overwhelming the surface-limited reconstruction mechanism and leading to irreversible deactivation.
- **The reaction must involve at least one reactant that can reversibly modify the surface chemical potential (e.g., O2, H2).** — The feedback loop requires a species that can both induce and be removed by a surface phase change. Inert reactants or those forming strongly bound, irreversible surface species cannot drive the cycle.
- **The characteristic diffusion length of the driving intermediate on the surface must be comparable to or larger than the nanoparticle size.** — To establish a coherent oscillatory state across a nanoparticle, the surface must be able to communicate the change in chemical potential rapidly. Localized, uncoupled oscillations would not constitute an effective self-repair mechanism for the entire particle.

### 1.5 Cadre theorique

Non-linear Dynamical Systems Theory applied to heterogeneous catalysis, specifically the theory of kinetic phase transitions and adsorbate-induced surface reconstructions leading to isothermal rate oscillations (e.g., Krischer, Eiswirth, Ertl framework).

## 4. Protocole experimental

**Timeline globale**: 9-21 months
**Budget global**: €20k-120k

### 4.1 Phase 1 — In Silico Validation

**Objectif**: To rapidly test the core dynamical systems hypothesis using computational models, identifying if the proposed negative feedback loop can theoretically generate stable limit cycles under the specified boundary conditions.

**Methodologie**: 1. **Microkinetic Modeling:** Develop a mean-field microkinetic model (e.g., in Python using SciPy/NumPy or COPASI) incorporating the catalytic cycle (e.g., CH4 oxidation on Ni) and a reversible surface reconstruction step. The reconstruction rate will be modeled as a function of a key intermediate coverage (e.g., O*), with activation energies (Ea) for forward (metallic to oxide) and reverse steps set within the 0.7-1.2 eV range.
2. **Dynamical Systems Analysis:** Perform stability and bifurcation analysis (using PyDSTool or XPPAUT) on the coupled ordinary differential equations (ODEs) for surface coverages and active site density (θ). Parameter sweeps will be conducted over the independent variables (T, pressure ratio, composition).
3. **Agent-Based Simulation (Optional):** Use Kinetic Monte Carlo (kMC) simulations (e.g., with Zacros) on a simplified lattice to assess the coherence requirement across nanoparticle facets.

- Cout: €0-2k (primarily compute time if using cloud/HPC)
- Duree: 4-8 weeks
- Equipement: High-performance computing cluster or powerful workstation
- Logiciels: Python (SciPy, NumPy, Matplotlib, PyDSTool), COPASI, Zacros (optional), VESTA/OVITO for visualization

**Criteres de succes :**

- Existence of a stable limit cycle in the ODE model: Model produces sustained oscillations (non-damping) for >100 cycles within the predicted τ range (10-1000 s) for at least one set of parameters within the specified variable ranges.
- Sensitivity to key intermediate: Forcing constant, saturating coverage of the driving intermediate (O*) in the model collapses the oscillation amplitude (Δθ) to < 0.01 ML.

- **GO**: Both success criteria are met. The model robustly predicts limit cycles under plausible conditions, validating the theoretical feasibility of the mechanism.
- **NO-GO**: No parameter set yields stable oscillations, or oscillations only occur outside all specified boundary conditions (e.g., above Tammann temperature). The core feedback loop is theoretically invalid.
- **PIVOT**: Oscillations require unrealistic parameters (Ea > 1.5 eV, coupling strength not physically plausible). The hypothesis may need reformulation (e.g., different intermediate, modified mechanism).

### 4.2 Phase 2 — Minimal Experimental Validation

**Objectif**: To physically confirm the existence of coupled reaction-rate and surface-structure oscillations on a single, well-defined model catalyst nanoparticle.

**Methodologie**: 1. **Sample Preparation:** Synthesize or purchase commercial Ni nanoparticles (5-20 nm) supported on SiO2 (TEM grids).
2. **In Situ Gas Cell STEM/EELS:** Use an Environmental Transmission Electron Microscope (ETEM) with a gas cell (e.g., Protochips Atmosphere) and Electron Energy Loss Spectroscopy (EELS).
   - **Conditions:** 600 K, CH4:O2 mixture (ratio 2:1, total pressure ~1-5 mbar).
   - **Measurement:** Acquire rapid-scan EELS spectra (exposure time < 1s) focused on the Ni L3-edge white-line ratio, a proxy for oxidation state and thus active site density (θ). Acquire simultaneously: (i) real-time video for particle morphology, (ii) EELS spectra every 5-10 seconds for 30+ minutes.
3. **Parallel Mass Spectrometry:** Connect the gas cell effluent to a residual gas analyzer (RGA) or micro-GC to correlate local surface state with global reaction rate (TOF for CO2/H2O production).

- Cout: €10k-15k (primarily instrument time, 3-5 days of ETEM access)
- Duree: 2-3 months (including prep, measurement, analysis)
- Equipement: Environmental TEM with EELS capability and gas cell, Mass Spectrometer (RGA) or micro-Gas Chromatograph, Glove box for sample prep
- Logiciels: DigitalMicrograph (Gatan) for EELS acquisition, Hyperspy for EELS analysis, Custom Python scripts for time-series analysis

**Criteres de succes :**

- Observation of anti-phase oscillations: Clear, periodic oscillation in the Ni L3 white-line ratio with an amplitude corresponding to Δθ ≥ 0.05 ML, and an anti-phase oscillation in the CO2 production rate (MS signal).
- Perturbation response (Proof of mechanism): A brief (5s) O2 pulse during a metallic (low white-line) phase causes a premature shift to the oxidized state, advancing the cycle by ≥20% of the period.

- **GO**: Both success criteria are met. The core oscillatory mechanism is experimentally confirmed on a single-particle level.
- **NO-GO**: No oscillations are observed in either structure or rate after testing across a range of conditions (T, P ratio) within the boundary limits. The phenomenon does not exist in the chosen model system.
- **PIVOT**: Oscillations are observed but are not anti-phase, or are not coherent across the particle. The mechanism may differ (e.g., driven by carbon, not oxygen). Focus Phase 3 on characterizing the actual driver.

### 4.3 Phase 3 — Full Experimental Protocol

**Objectif**: To rigorously quantify the self-repair (stability) benefit, establish the operational window, and validate the falsifiable predictions for publication.

**Methodologie**: 1. **Long-Term Stability Quantification:**
   - Use a multi-channel, parallel reactor system (e.g., from PID Eng & Tech) with online GC.
   - Run two identical Ni/SiO2 catalysts for 100+ hours: one under oscillatory conditions (identified in Phase 2), one under static, non-oscillatory but time-averaged identical conditions (e.g., slightly different T or P ratio that yields steady state).
   - Periodically (every 10-20h), sample catalysts for ex situ characterization (XPS, TEM) to measure mean active site density <θ> via Ni0/Ni2+ ratio and particle size.
2. **Operational Window Mapping:**
   - Use high-throughput AP-XPS (Near Ambient Pressure XPS) at a synchrotron (e.g., BESSY II, MAX IV) or lab-based system.
   - Automate rapid T and gas ratio sweeps while monitoring Ni 2p and O 1s spectra with ~1 min time resolution to map the (T, P) phase space where oscillations occur.
3. **Advanced In Situ Characterization:**
   - Perform Phase 2-type ETEM/EELS experiments on a composition gradient sample (Ni-Cu alloy) to test Variable 3.
   - Use in situ XRD or quick-XAS at a synchrotron to confirm reconstruction coherence across an ensemble of particles.

- Cout: €50k-100k+ (major costs: synchrotron beamtime, reactor system operation, personnel)
- Duree: 6-12 months
- Equipement: Parallel reactor system with online GC, AP-XPS instrument (synchrotron beamtime preferred), ETEM/EELS (additional time), XPS, TEM, XRD
- Logiciels: High-throughput data analysis pipelines (Python), SPECS Lab Pro for AP-XPS, OriginPro/Prism for statistics

**Criteres de succes :**

- Degradation rate reduction (Prediction 1): The oscillatory catalyst shows a ≥50% lower degradation rate (d<θ>/dt) over 100h compared to the static control (p < 0.05 in one-tailed t-test).
- Operational window: Oscillations are observed over a contiguous region in (T, P) space spanning at least 50 K and one order of magnitude in pressure ratio.
- Coherence requirement (Boundary Condition 3): Oscillations are coherent (in-phase) across individual nanoparticles > 10 nm, but decoherent or absent in particles < 5 nm.

- **GO**: All success criteria are met. The hypothesis is fully validated, demonstrating a novel, useful self-repair mechanism.
- **NO-GO**: Prediction 1 is falsified: oscillations do not confer a stability benefit. The phenomenon is a scientific curiosity but not a viable self-repair strategy.
- **PIVOT**: Stability benefit exists but the operational window is extremely narrow. Pivot focus to catalyst design (alloying, support) to widen the window, rather than fundamental mechanism validation.

## 7. Gap Manifest residuel

### 7.1 Data gaps

- Lack of demonstrated 'self-repair' outcome. Existing papers show oscillatory behavior or periodic regeneration, but not clear evidence that the oscillatory mechanism preferentially restores *degraded* or *poisoned* sites to an active state, effectively extending catalyst lifetime beyond normal deactivation.
- Limited generality. Evidence is strong for oxidation reactions (CH4, H2) on late transition metals (Ni, Rh). It is unclear if the mechanism applies to other reaction classes (e.g., hydrogenation, reforming) or different catalyst materials (oxides, sulfides).
- Mechanistic understanding of the feedback trigger. While papers show oscillations, the precise molecular-scale trigger that initiates the reconstruction phase (e.g., a critical coverage of a reaction intermediate) is often not fully elucidated for each system.
