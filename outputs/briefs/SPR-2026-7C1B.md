## Abstract

If the Fourier components V(q) of a spatially-modulated synthetic potential in an optical lattice are engineered to match a target non-local interaction profile, derived from an inverse-design algorithm adapted from quasi-phase-matched nonlinear optics, then the measured static structure factor S(q) and pair correlation function C_pair(r) will exhibit quantitative enhancement specifically at the target wavevectors q_target, because the engineered potential directly renormalizes the effective electron-electron interaction vertex, promoting the formation of correlations with the prescribed non-local symmetry.

The proposed mechanism involves 4 causal steps: (1) A target effective extended Hubbard interaction term, H_target = Σ_{i,j} V_ij n_ -> (2) Using an inverse-design algorithm adapted from QPM optics, a spatial modulation  -> (3) This engineered potential is applied to a fermionic quantum gas in the lattice,  -> (4) The renormalized Hamiltonian drives the system towards a ground state with enhan.

Literature grounding on 3 verified references yields a novelty score of 0.8 (novel). A 3-phase experimental protocol (budget: €20k-80k, timeline: 8-14 months) is proposed, starting with in silico validation. A panel of 5 expert reviewers reached a consensus score of 5.5/10.

## 1. Hypothese et mecanisme propose

### 1.1 Formulation formelle

If the Fourier components V(q) of a spatially-modulated synthetic potential in an optical lattice are engineered to match a target non-local interaction profile, derived from an inverse-design algorithm adapted from quasi-phase-matched nonlinear optics, then the measured static structure factor S(q) and pair correlation function C_pair(r) will exhibit quantitative enhancement specifically at the target wavevectors q_target, because the engineered potential directly renormalizes the effective electron-electron interaction vertex, promoting the formation of correlations with the prescribed non-local symmetry.

### 1.3 Chaine causale

1. Step 1: A target effective extended Hubbard interaction term, H_target = Σ_{i,j} V_ij n_i n_j with a specific non-local profile (e.g., dominant nearest-neighbor repulsion/attraction), is decomposed into its Fourier components V(q_target).
1. Step 2: Using an inverse-design algorithm adapted from QPM optics, a spatial modulation pattern for the optical lattice depth (or a coupled auxiliary lattice) is computed, whose Fourier spectrum V_k directly maps to the target V(q_target) when projected onto the lowest Bloch band.
1. Step 3: This engineered potential is applied to a fermionic quantum gas in the lattice, renormalizing the bare contact interactions and introducing the target non-local interaction vertex into the low-energy effective Hamiltonian.
1. Step 4: The renormalized Hamiltonian drives the system towards a ground state with enhanced density-density or pair correlations at the wavevectors q_target, which is detected via quantum gas microscopy and time-of-flight measurements.

**Hypotheses cles :**

- The system remains in the lowest Bloch band (valid for modulation amplitudes V_k < 0.3 E_r and lattice depths > 5 E_r).
- The engineered potential does not induce significant heating or decoherence on the timescale required for adiabatic state preparation (~100-500 ms).
- The dominant effect of the potential modulation is to engineer interactions, not single-particle dispersion (valid for fillings n=0.4-0.6 where the Fermi surface is not nested with the modulation period).
- The on-site interaction U is sufficiently strong (U/J > 4) to suppress trivial band insulating phases but not dominant enough to completely overwhelm the engineered non-local terms.

**Inconnues identifiees :**

- The precise quantitative relationship between the Fourier amplitude V_k and the renormalized interaction strength V_eff(q) in the presence of strong correlations.
- The maximum complexity (number of independent Fourier components) of an interaction profile that can be engineered before coherence limits are reached.
- The full scaling of adiabatic preparation time with system size for these non-local Hamiltonians.

### 1.4 Conditions aux limites

- **The temperature of the quantum gas must be below T_c = 0.2 J/k_B.** — Above this temperature, thermal fluctuations will wash out the signature of engineered quantum correlations, rendering S(q) measurements insensitive to the interaction renormalization.
- **The total experimental sequence time (preparation + modulation + measurement) must be less than the coherence time T_coherence, estimated at < 1.5 s.** — Beyond T_coherence, heating and dephasing will degrade the many-body state fidelity, confounding the measurement of engineered correlations. In-situ monitoring of double occupancy and entropy will be used to track this.
- **The filling factor must be controlled within Δn = ±0.05 of the target value (e.g., n=0.5±0.05).** — Significant deviations from the target filling will alter the Fermi surface and the phase space for the engineered interactions, potentially driving the system into a different competing phase (e.g., a density wave).

### 1.5 Cadre theorique

Extended Hubbard Model with Engineered Non-Local Interactions, analyzed via Fourier-based Inverse-Design principles transferred from Nonlinear Optics (Quasi-Phase-Matching theory).

## 4. Protocole experimental

**Timeline globale**: 8-14 months
**Budget global**: €20k-80k

### 4.1 Phase 1 — In Silico Validation

**Objectif**: To computationally test the core assumption that a spatially-modulated lattice potential can renormalize the effective extended Hubbard interaction V_eff(q) as predicted, and to identify the most promising parameter set (V_k, q_target) for experimental test.

**Methodologie**: 1. Implement a band-projection calculation (tight-binding model) to map the engineered lattice modulation V(x) = Σ_k V_k cos(kx + φ_k) to an effective non-local interaction term V_ij in the lowest Bloch band. Validate against known limits (e.g., constant potential). 2. Use exact diagonalization (ED) for small systems (e.g., 1D chain, 8-12 sites) via the QuSpin (Python) library to compute the ground state for the renormalized Hamiltonian H = H_kin + U Σ_i n_i↑n_i↓ + Σ_{i≠j} V_ij n_i n_j. 3. Compute the predicted static structure factor S(q) and pair correlation C_pair(r) from the ED ground state. 4. Perform a parameter sweep over V_k (0.0-0.3 E_r) and q_target (0, π/2, π) for fixed U/J=6, n=0.5. Compare results to control (V_k=0).

- Cout: €0-2k (cloud computing if needed)
- Duree: 4-6 weeks
- Equipement: High-performance workstation or small computing cluster (e.g., 32+ cores, 128GB RAM)
- Logiciels: QuSpin, NumPy/SciPy, Matplotlib for visualization, Jupyter Notebooks for documentation

**Criteres de succes :**

- Predicted enhancement ΔS(q_target) for V_k=0.2 E_r, q_target=π: ΔS(q=π) > +0.10 (from ED baseline)
- Specificity of the effect: |ΔS(q_non-target)| / |ΔS(q_target)| < 0.3

- **GO**: Both success criteria are met for at least one parameter set, confirming the theoretical mechanism is sound and the predicted effect size is within measurable bounds.
- **NO-GO**: ΔS(q_target) < +0.05 for all tested V_k up to 0.3 E_r, indicating the renormalization mechanism is too weak to be detectable experimentally.
- **PIVOT**: Effect is present but requires V_k > 0.25 E_r or shows strong sensitivity to filling n. Pivot to re-run Phase 1 with modified boundary conditions (e.g., different n, U/J) or explore a different class of modulation patterns (e.g., sub-wavelength lattices).

### 4.2 Phase 2 — Minimal Experimental Validation

**Objectif**: To perform the smallest-scale cold-atom experiment to detect the predicted enhancement of S(q=π) with an engineered V_{q=π} potential, using a simplified 1D lattice configuration.

**Methodologie**: 1. Use an existing 1D optical lattice setup for fermionic 6Li or 40K. 2. Prepare a degenerate gas at T < 0.2 J/k_B, n ≈ 0.5, in a deep 3D lattice with weak 1D tunneling (J/h ~ 50 Hz). 3. Implement the engineered potential via an additional, intensity-modulated laser beam creating a standing wave with period 2a_lat (i.e., wavevector π/a). Use an acousto-optic modulator (AOM) driven by an arbitrary waveform generator (AWG) to control V_k. 4. Adiabatically ramp on the modulation to final V_k = 0.2 E_r over 100 ms. 5. Measure S(q) via band-mapping time-of-flight (TOF) absorption imaging: suddenly release the atoms, and measure the density variance in momentum space ⟨|n(q)|^2⟩, which relates to S(q). 6. Compare 30 runs with modulation ON (V_k=0.2) to 30 runs OFF (V_k=0).

- Cout: €8k-15k (for additional modulation laser, AOM, AWG if not available)
- Duree: 2-3 months
- Equipement: Existing quantum gas microscope or standard absorption imaging setup, Additional 1064nm or 532nm laser & AOM for potential modulation, High-speed AWG (e.g., Spectrum Instrumentation M4i.6622), PID-stabilized laser intensity controller
- Logiciels: Camera acquisition software (e.g., LabView, Python), Analysis pipelines for TOF images (e.g., MATLab, Python with OpenCV)

**Criteres de succes :**

- Measured ΔS(q=π): ΔS(q=π) > +0.08 (with p < 0.01 in one-tailed Welch's t-test)
- Specificity: |ΔS(q=π/2)| < 0.03

- **GO**: Success criteria are met, confirming the core experimental effect. Proceed to full 2D validation with quantum gas microscopy.
- **NO-GO**: No significant change in S(q=π) (p > 0.05) after optimizing adiabatic ramp and confirming potential modulation calibration. Suggests a fundamental flaw in the mechanism or insurmountable technical noise.
- **PIVOT**: Effect is seen but is smaller than predicted (ΔS ~ 0.04-0.07) or shows unexpected heating. Pivot to Phase 3 but with a focus on improving adiabatic preparation, exploring different atom species, or reducing disorder.

### 4.3 Phase 3 — Full Experimental Protocol

**Objectif**: To provide rigorous, publication-quality validation of the inverse-design concept by measuring both S(q) and C_pair(r) in a 2D system with site-resolved microscopy, for multiple target wavevectors.

**Methodologie**: 1. Use a quantum gas microscope with single-site resolution for fermions (e.g., 6Li with Raman sideband cooling). 2. Implement 2D Fourier-engineered potentials using a digital micromirror device (DMD) or a spatial light modulator (SLM) to shape the intensity profile of a lattice laser, creating V(x,y) with multiple Fourier components. 3. Prepare a low-entropy 2D Fermi-Hubbard system at n=0.5, U/J=6, T < 0.2 J/k_B. 4. Adiabatically apply the engineered potential. 5. Measure: a) S(q) via site-resolved parity-projection and calculation of density-density correlations. b) C_pair(r) via doublon-holon correlations after a deep-lattice ramp. 6. Systematically test 3-4 different target interaction profiles (e.g., nearest-neighbor repulsion (q=(π,π)), checkerboard (q=(π,0)), and a more complex pattern). Compare to ab initio numerical simulations (DMRG/PEPS for 2D).

- Cout: €50k-150k+ (primarily for DMD/SLM system, laser upgrades, and personnel)
- Duree: 6-12 months
- Equipement: Quantum gas microscope setup for fermions, High-resolution DMD or SLM (e.g., Hamamatsu X10468 or Meadowlark Optics 1920x1152), Additional high-power laser line for DMD/SLM projection, Ultra-stable magnetic field environment
- Logiciels: Inverse-design algorithm (from Phase 1) extended to 2D, Microscope image analysis pipeline (e.g., single-atom detection via neural networks), Tensor network simulation code (e.g., TeNPy, SyTen) for 2D comparisons

**Criteres de succes :**

- Enhancement of S(q_target): ΔS(q_target) > +0.15 for at least two distinct target wavevectors, with statistical significance p < 0.01.
- Corresponding change in C_pair(r): ΔC_pair(r=nearest-neighbor) > +0.04 and ΔC_pair(r=0) < -0.03 for the q=(π,π) target.
- Agreement with theory: Quantitative agreement (within 20%) between measured ΔS(q) and ab initio tensor network simulations for the same parameters.

- **GO**: All success criteria are met, validating the hypothesis. The project culminates in a publication and opens a new research direction.
- **NO-GO**: Despite successful Phase 2, the 2D site-resolved measurements show no effect or effects inconsistent with predictions, even after exhaustive optimization. This would invalidate the hypothesis for 2D or realistic systems.
- **PIVOT**: Targeted enhancement is observed but is accompanied by strong, unexpected competing orders (e.g., phase separation). Pivot the research focus to studying the competition between engineered and emergent interactions.
