### 1.3 Chaine causale

1. Step 1: A spatially variable ice crystal fabric (c-axis orientation) and/or the presence of water-filled fractures induces local anisotropy and reductions in the effective shear modulus (μ) and increases anelastic attenuation (Qs⁻¹).
1. Step 2: These mechanical perturbations alter the propagation characteristics of seismic shear waves (S-waves), specifically reducing their phase velocity (Vs = √(μ/ρ)) and increasing their amplitude decay with distance, relative to isotropic, cold ice.
1. Step 3: A viscoelastic FWI algorithm, which minimizes the difference between observed and synthetic seismic waveforms by iteratively updating a 3D model of μ and Qs⁻¹, will converge to a solution where regions of low Vs and high Qs⁻¹ spatially correlate with independently mapped fabric clusters or fracture zones.

**Hypotheses cles :**

- The seismic wavefield in the frequency band 10-200 Hz is primarily sensitive to elastic and anelastic properties, with scattering effects from discrete fractures being treatable as an effective medium attenuation.
- The starting velocity model for FWI (e.g., from travel-time tomography) is accurate enough to avoid cycle-skipping for the lowest frequencies used (10-30 Hz).
- The source wavelet and receiver coupling are sufficiently well-characterized to allow accurate forward modeling.
- The ice can be approximated as a transversely isotropic, viscoelastic solid at the seismic wavelength scale (≈15-150 m).

**Inconnues identifiees :**

- The precise quantitative relationship between specific fabric eigenvalues (e.g., from eigenvalue analysis of c-axis orientations) and the anisotropic seismic tensor in temperate ice.
- The relative contribution of fracture water content versus fabric to the total observed seismic attenuation (Qs⁻¹) in the 10-200 Hz band.
- The minimum fracture density and scale required to be detectable as a perturbation in effective medium properties versus as discrete scatterers.

### 1.4 Conditions aux limites

- **Ice temperature must be isothermal and very close to the pressure melting point (e.g., -2°C to 0°C).** — This minimizes the strong confounding effect of a vertical temperature gradient on the seismic velocity profile, allowing fabric and fracture signals to be isolated. Applicable to temperate or polythermal glaciers in ablation zones.
- **Survey depth of investigation is limited to the upper 200 m of ice.** — Seismic attenuation in ice significantly reduces signal-to-noise ratio for frequencies >10 Hz at greater depths, limiting the resolution of FWI which requires high-frequency data.
- **Minimum source-receiver offset must be ≥5 times the target depth.** — To ensure sufficient penetration and wide-angle coverage necessary for constraining velocity variations via FWI, not just reflection imaging.
- **Initial model for FWI must be derived from first-arrival travel-time tomography of the same dataset.** — This ensures the starting model is data-consistent and minimizes the risk of cycle-skipping, a primary failure mode of FWI.

## 4. Protocole experimental

**Timeline globale**: 9-21 months
**Budget global**: €20k-120k

### 4.1 Phase 1 — In Silico Validation

**Objectif**: To determine if the proposed viscoelastic FWI workflow can theoretically resolve 10m-scale anomalies in shear modulus (μ) and attenuation (Qs⁻¹) under realistic noise conditions and starting model errors, and to identify the most critical acquisition parameters.

**Methodologie**: 1. **Synthetic Model Construction**: Use MATLAB/Python with SPECFEM2D/3D or Salvus (if available) to create a 200m x 200m x 200m 3D viscoelastic, transversely isotropic (VTI) model. Embed known anomalies: a) A 10m-thick layer with a 5% Vs reduction and 50% Qs⁻¹ increase (simulating a fracture zone). b) A 20m-wide column with 3% azimuthal Vs variation (cos2θ pattern) representing a fabric transition. 2. **Forward Modeling & Inversion**: Generate synthetic shot gathers (10-200 Hz Ricker wavelet) for dense 2D/3D acquisition geometries (e.g., 5m receiver spacing, shots every 20m). Add realistic Gaussian noise (SNR=10). Perform FWI using an open-source code (e.g., Devito, SeisFlows) or a custom gradient-based algorithm (L-BFGS). Start from a smooth version of the true model perturbed with 5% velocity error. 3. **Sensitivity Analysis**: Test recovery of anomalies vs. key parameters: maximum source frequency (50 vs. 200 Hz), offset/depth ratio, and initial model accuracy.

- Cout: €0-2k (cloud computing credits if no local HPC)
- Duree: 4-8 weeks
- Equipement: High-performance computing node (≥32 cores, 128GB RAM)
- Logiciels: Python/NumPy/SciPy, Devito (FWI framework), ObsPy (seismic processing), ParaView/Matplotlib (visualization)

**Criteres de succes :**

- Anomaly Recovery Metric (ARM): ARM ≥ 0.7 for both Vs and Qs⁻¹ anomalies. ARM = 1 - (||m_true - m_rec|| / ||m_true - m_background||).
- Misfit Reduction: Final normalized L2 waveform misfit ≤ 25% of initial misfit for the optimal acquisition scenario.
- Azimuthal Pattern Correlation: R² ≥ 0.8 for cos(2θ) fit to recovered Vs in the fabric anomaly column.

- **GO**: All three success criteria are met with the 'optimal' acquisition geometry (e.g., 5m receivers, 200 Hz max freq). This demonstrates theoretical feasibility.
- **NO-GO**: ARM < 0.4 for both parameters even under ideal noise-free, perfect-starting-model conditions. The physics of the problem is likely intractable with the proposed method.
- **PIVOT**: ARM for Qs⁻¹ is poor (<0.5) but good for Vs (>0.7). Pivot Phase 2 to focus solely on elastic FWI for fabric, dropping the attenuation/fracture hypothesis.

### 4.2 Phase 2 — Minimal Experimental Validation

**Objectif**: To perform a field pilot on a readily accessible temperate glacier to confirm that the seismic wavefield contains detectable signals from fabric/fracture heterogeneity and that the FWI workflow converges on field data.

**Methodologie**: 1. **Site Selection & Reconnaissance**: Select a small, well-studied temperate alpine glacier (e.g., Argentière Glacier, France; Hintereisferner, Austria; or a Svalbard glacier) with known structural heterogeneity from prior GPR/cores. 2. **Minimal Acquisition**: Deploy a single, dense 2D seismic line (300-500m length). Use a lightweight seismic source (e.g., Betsy Gun, 8lb sledgehammer on plate, or small explosive charges) and 48-96 10Hz geophones at 5m spacing. Record shots every 20m. Simultaneously collect a co-located high-frequency GPR profile (100-200 MHz) to identify fracture reflections. 3. **Data Processing & Inversion**: Process data (filtering, gain, geometry assignment). Perform first-arrival travel-time tomography (using PyGIMLi or TOMOFAST) to create the FWI starting model. Execute the viscoelastic FWI workflow validated in Phase 1, but limited to 2D and lower frequencies (10-80 Hz). 4. **Validation**: Compare FWI-derived Vs/Qs⁻¹ sections with GPR reflectivity and any available ice core fabric data (from literature or a single new shallow core).

- Cout: €8k-15k (field logistics, equipment rental, travel)
- Duree: 2-3 months (1-2 weeks field, rest processing)
- Equipement: 48-96 10Hz vertical geophones, Geode or Summit II seismograph, Betsy Gun or hammer/plate source, 100-200 MHz GPR system (e.g., MALÅ ProEx), GPS units, Field computing kit
- Logiciels: SeisSpace/ProMAX or ObsPy for processing, Devito/PyGIMLi for inversion, ReflexW for GPR processing

**Criteres de succes :**

- Field Data Misfit Reduction: Normalized L2 misfit ≤ 60% of initial misfit (from travel-time tomography model).
- Spatial Correlation with GPR: Positive spatial cross-correlation coefficient > 0.3 between Qs⁻¹ and GPR envelope amplitude at predicted depth of fractures.
- Model Convergence: Inversion converges stably (monotonic misfit decrease) without manual intervention for >50 iterations.

- **GO**: Misfit reduction ≥40% AND spatial correlation >0.3. The field data contains a usable signal and the workflow functions.
- **NO-GO**: Misfit reduction <20% OR data is dominated by source/ coupling noise making waveforms unusable for FWI. The fundamental data requirement is not met.
- **PIVOT**: Good Vs recovery but poor/no Qs⁻¹ recovery. Pivot Phase 3 to elastic FWI only, using amplitude decay for qualitative fracture mapping.

### 4.3 Phase 3 — Full Experimental Protocol

**Objectif**: To rigorously validate the hypothesis by performing 3D viscoelastic FWI on a dense dataset from a characterized site, achieving the target resolution (≤10m horizontal, ≤5m vertical), and quantitatively testing the falsifiable predictions against ground truth.

**Methodologie**: 1. **Comprehensive 3D Survey Design**: Deploy a dense 3D grid (e.g., 10 lines, 100m apart, each with 5m receiver spacing) covering a ~500m x 500m area on a well-characterized glacier (e.g., ablation zone of a polythermal glacier). Use a more powerful source (e.g., IVI Minivib or larger explosives) for deeper penetration and higher frequencies. 2. **Multi-Method Ground Truthing**: Acquire concurrent 3D GPR. Drill 3-5 shallow ice cores (to 150m) along transects for direct fabric analysis (thin sections, eigenvalue analysis) and temperature measurement. 3. **Advanced 3D FWI**: Process the full dataset. Perform 3D first-arrival tomography. Execute large-scale 3D viscoelastic FWI on an HPC cluster, inverting for Vs and Qs⁻¹ simultaneously. 4. **Hypothesis Testing**: Conduct the statistical tests outlined in the falsifiable predictions: a) Regression of FWI-derived azimuthal Vs vs. core fabric direction. b) Welch's t-test on Qs⁻¹ near vs. far from GPR fractures. c) Misfit analysis on field vs. synthetic 'inverse crime' test.

- Cout: €80k-200k+ (major field campaign, coring, HPC, personnel)
- Duree: 9-15 months (3-month field season, 6-12 months processing/analysis)
- Equipement: ~500 geophones, Large-channel seismograph (e.g., 1000+ channels), IVI Minivib or equivalent seismic source, High-frequency 3D GPR array, Hot-water ice coring system, Field lab for thin-section preparation
- Logiciels: Commercial/advanced FWI software (e.g., Salvus, SPECFEM3D) for large 3D, Advanced seismic processing suite (e.g., SeisSpace), Petrel or equivalent for 3D volume interpretation

**Criteres de succes :**

- Prediction 1: Azimuthal Vs vs. Fabric: F-test p-value < 0.01 for cos(2θ) model, rejecting H0.
- Prediction 2: Qs⁻¹ vs. Fractures: Welch's t-test p-value < 0.05 (Bonferroni corrected), rejecting H0.
- Prediction 3: Misfit Reduction & Robustness: Field data misfit reduction significantly >60% (one-sample t-test p<0.05) AND synthetic 'complex physics' test misfit >15%.
- Spatial Resolution: Ability to resolve a 10m x 10m x 5m test anomaly in checkerboard tests performed on the real acquisition geometry.

- **GO**: At least two of the three primary predictions are confirmed (statistically significant) AND spatial resolution target is met. Hypothesis is substantially validated.
- **NO-GO**: All three primary predictions are rejected (null hypotheses cannot be rejected). The core hypothesis is falsified.
- **PIVOT**: Only one prediction is confirmed. Requires fundamental re-evaluation of the proposed causal mechanism (e.g., fractures may be detected but not via Qs⁻¹; fabric may influence attenuation more than velocity).
