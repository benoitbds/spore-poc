## 1. Hypothese et mecanisme propose

### 1.1 Formulation formelle

If mechanical vibration is applied to a continuous flow microreactor transporting a dense slurry, then the frequency of clogging events will decrease by 30-80% relative to non-vibrated conditions, because vibration disrupts force chains and arch formation at the constriction, as predicted by the dimensionless vibration intensity Γ and the orifice-to-particle diameter ratio D/d, provided the particle volume fraction φ remains above 0.45 and the Stokes number St < 10.

### 1.3 Chaine causale

1. Step 1: Vibration at frequency f and amplitude a imposes oscillatory acceleration on the granular bed, generating a dimensionless vibration intensity Γ = a·(2πf)²/g.
1. Step 2: When Γ exceeds a critical threshold (Γ_c ≈ 1-2 for non-cohesive spheres), the granular bed transitions from a jammed to a fluidized state, reducing inter-particle friction and breaking force chains that support arches.
1. Step 3: The reduced arch stability at the constriction (orifice diameter D) allows particles to flow through without forming a stable bridge, provided D/d > 3 (empirical hopper rule).
1. Step 4: For cohesive or polydisperse slurries, vibration also reduces the effective yield stress and disrupts particle aggregates, further suppressing clogging.

**Hypotheses cles :**

- Particles are approximately spherical and non-deformable (Young's modulus > 1 GPa).
- The carrier fluid is Newtonian and its viscosity is constant (±5%) over the experimental temperature range.
- The particle volume fraction φ remains constant throughout each experiment (no sedimentation).
- The microchannel walls are rigid and do not vibrate independently of the fluid.

**Inconnues identifiees :**

- We do not know the exact functional relationship between Γ_c and particle shape (sphericity).
- We do not know whether vibration-induced heating (Joule effect in piezoelectric actuators) affects fluid viscosity significantly.
- We do not know the scaling of clogging suppression efficiency from 2D Hele-Shaw cells to 3D cylindrical microchannels.

### 1.4 Conditions aux limites

- **Particle volume fraction φ must remain between 0.30 and 0.55.** — Below 0.30, the slurry is too dilute for granular jamming to dominate (hydrodynamic effects prevail); above 0.55, the system approaches random close packing and vibration may not fluidize the bed.
- **Stokes number St must be < 10.** — For St > 10, particle inertia dominates and vibration-induced collisions may cause fragmentation or erosion, altering particle size distribution.
- **Channel Reynolds number Re_channel < 100.** — Above Re=100, inertial effects in the fluid (secondary flows, Dean vortices) may disrupt the granular flow pattern and invalidate the analogy with dry granular hoppers.
- **Particle-particle friction coefficient μ_pp must be between 0.1 and 0.6.** — For μ_pp < 0.1, particles are too slippery to form stable arches even without vibration; for μ_pp > 0.6, cohesive forces (van der Waals) become significant and require additional modeling.

### 1.5 Cadre theorique

Granular mechanics and jamming phase transitions (Liu & Nagel, 1998); vibration-induced fluidization (Jaeger et al., 1996); hopper discharge scaling laws (Beverloo et al., 1961; Nedderman, 1992).

## 4. Protocole experimental

**Timeline globale**: 8-14 months
**Budget global**: €20k-80k

### 4.1 Phase 1 — In Silico Validation (Discrete Element Method - DEM + CFD)

**Objectif**: To rapidly falsify the core mechanism (Γ threshold, D/d effect) via simulation before any physical experiments, minimizing cost and time.

**Methodologie**: Perform 2D and 3D Discrete Element Method (DEM) simulations coupled with Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) using LIGGGHTS (open-source) and OpenFOAM (open-source) or commercial Altair EDEM. Simulate a microchannel constriction (orifice) with spherical particles (monodisperse and polydisperse) in a Newtonian fluid. Vary Γ (0.5-10), D/d (2-6), φ (0.45-0.55), and St (0.1-10). Measure clogging frequency, time-to-clog, and force chain networks (using DEM post-processing). Run 10 replicates per condition (each replicate = 10 seconds of simulated flow). Use HPC cluster (e.g., local university cluster or cloud AWS/GCP).

- Cout: €500-2000
- Duree: 4-6 weeks
- Equipement: HPC cluster (≥64 cores, 256 GB RAM), Local workstation for pre/post-processing (16+ cores, 64 GB RAM)
- Logiciels: LIGGGHTS (open-source DEM), OpenFOAM (open-source CFD), Paraview (visualization), Python (scripting, data analysis, scipy, numpy, matplotlib), MATLAB (optional for advanced statistics)

**Criteres de succes :**

- Clogging frequency reduction (Γ=5 vs. Γ=0.5 at D/d=4, φ=0.50): ≥50% reduction (simulated mean difference)
- Clogging frequency at D/d=5 vs. D/d=2.5 at Γ=3: Clogging frequency at D/d=5 < 0.1 events/hour (simulated) AND at D/d=2.5 > 5 events/hour (simulated)
- Interaction effect of oversized particles and Γ on clogging frequency: Two-way ANOVA p-value for interaction term < 0.05 (simulated data)

- **GO**: All three success criteria are met (simulated predictions match expected bounds).
- **NO-GO**: Any of the three success criteria fails (e.g., no significant reduction in clogging with Γ, or D/d effect absent).
- **PIVOT**: Partial success: e.g., Prediction 1 holds but Prediction 2 fails. Then refine D/d range or particle friction model in simulation before proceeding to Phase 2.

### 4.2 Phase 2 — Minimal Experimental Validation (Single-Channel Microfluidic Device)

**Objectif**: To test the core mechanism (vibration-induced jamming suppression) in a simple, low-cost physical system using a single microchannel with a constriction.

**Methodologie**: Fabricate a single microchannel (PDMS or glass, width=500 µm, depth=500 µm) with a constriction (D=100-300 µm) using soft lithography or laser cutting. Use monodisperse glass beads (d=50 µm, CV<5%) suspended in a density-matched Newtonian fluid (e.g., water-glycerol mixture, viscosity ~10 mPa·s, ρ=2500 kg/m³ matched with CsCl or sodium polytungstate). Mount the device on a piezoelectric shaker (e.g., PiezoDrive PDS200) driven by a function generator (e.g., Keysight 33500B). Vary Γ (0.5, 2, 5) and D/d (2.5, 4, 5) at φ=0.50 and St<10. Use a high-speed camera (e.g., Phantom VEO 710, 1000 fps) to record flow at the constriction for 1 hour per condition (3 replicates per condition = 27 experiments). Automate image analysis (MATLAB or Python with OpenCV) to detect clogging events (defined as flow cessation >1s).

- Cout: €8k-15k
- Duree: 2-3 months
- Equipement: Microfluidic device (PDMS or glass, single channel with constriction), Syringe pump (e.g., Harvard Apparatus PHD Ultra), Piezoelectric shaker (e.g., PiezoDrive PDS200, 0-200 V, 0-10 kHz), Function generator (e.g., Keysight 33500B), High-speed camera (≥1000 fps, e.g., Phantom VEO 710 or Photron FASTCAM Mini AX), Microscope (inverted, 10x objective), Computer for data acquisition and analysis, Ultrasonic bath for particle dispersion, Viscosimeter (e.g., Brookfield DV2T)
- Logiciels: MATLAB (Image Processing Toolbox) or Python (OpenCV, scikit-image), LabVIEW (optional for automation), Micro-Manager (camera control)

**Criteres de succes :**

- Clogging frequency reduction (Γ=5 vs. Γ=0.5 at D/d=4): ≥50% reduction (mean of 3 replicates) AND p-value < 0.05 (one-tailed t-test)
- Clogging frequency at D/d=5 vs. D/d=2.5 at Γ=3: Clogging frequency at D/d=5 < 0.5 events/hour AND at D/d=2.5 > 5 events/hour (mean of 3 replicates)
- Reproducibility across replicates: Coefficient of variation (CV) of clogging frequency < 30% for each condition

- **GO**: All three success criteria are met (experimental data supports simulation predictions).
- **NO-GO**: Any criterion fails (e.g., no significant reduction, poor reproducibility, or D/d effect absent).
- **PIVOT**: Partial success: e.g., reduction observed but not statistically significant. Increase replicates (n=5) or adjust Γ range (e.g., test Γ=1-10).

### 4.3 Phase 3 — Full Experimental Protocol (Multi-Channel, Polydisperse, and Realistic Conditions)

**Objectif**: To rigorously validate the hypothesis under realistic conditions (polydisperse particles, continuous operation, multiple channel geometries) and produce publication-quality data.

**Methodologie**: Use a multi-channel microreactor (4 parallel channels, each with independent constriction) fabricated in glass (by wet etching or commercial supplier like Micronit). Use polydisperse glass beads (CV=10%, d_mean=50 µm) and also test with industrial slurry (e.g., silica in water, φ=0.45-0.55). Vary Γ (0.5, 2, 5, 10), D/d (2, 3, 4, 5, 6), φ (0.45, 0.50, 0.55), and St (0.5, 5, 10) in a full factorial design (n=5 per condition = 4×5×3×3×5 = 900 experiments, reduced to 180 by fractional factorial design). Use automated data acquisition (LabVIEW) to record pressure drop (pressure sensors, e.g., Honeywell 26PC), flow rate (flow sensor, e.g., Sensirion SLI-1000), and high-speed video (one channel at a time). Run each condition for 2 hours or until 10 clogging events occur. Perform particle tracking velocimetry (PTV) to measure velocity fluctuations near the constriction. Analyze effluent for particle size distribution (Malvern Mastersizer) to detect retention of oversized particles. Use statistical analysis (ANOVA, mixed-effects models) to test all three predictions.

- Cout: €40k-80k
- Duree: 6-9 months
- Equipement: Multi-channel glass microreactor (4 channels, custom design), Syringe pumps (4x, e.g., Harvard Apparatus PHD Ultra), Piezoelectric shaker array (4x, e.g., PiezoDrive PDS200), Function generator (4-channel, e.g., Keysight 33600A), High-speed camera (same as Phase 2, or higher resolution), Microscope with motorized stage (for multi-channel imaging), Pressure sensors (4x, 0-100 mbar, e.g., Honeywell 26PC), Flow sensors (4x, e.g., Sensirion SLI-1000), Data acquisition system (NI cDAQ-9178 with modules), Particle size analyzer (e.g., Malvern Mastersizer 3000), Rheometer (e.g., TA Instruments DHR-2) for slurry characterization, Temperature-controlled enclosure (±0.1°C)
- Logiciels: LabVIEW (automation and data acquisition), MATLAB (image processing, PTV, statistical analysis), Python (data analysis, visualization), ANSYS Fluent or COMSOL (optional for CFD validation)

**Criteres de succes :**

- Prediction 1: Clogging frequency reduction (Γ=5 vs. Γ=0.5 at D/d=4, φ=0.50): ≥50% reduction (mean) AND p-value < 0.01 (one-tailed t-test, Bonferroni corrected)
- Prediction 2: Clogging frequency at D/d=5 vs. D/d=2.5 at Γ=3: Clogging frequency at D/d=5 < 0.1 events/hour AND at D/d=2.5 > 5 events/hour (mean) AND Mann-Whitney p < 0.01
- Prediction 3: Interaction effect of oversized particles and Γ: Two-way ANOVA interaction term p < 0.01 AND post-hoc Tukey HSD shows significant mitigation at Γ=6
- Continuous operation demonstration: At least one condition (Γ≥5, D/d≥5, φ=0.50, St<10) achieves <0.1 clogging events/hour over 10 hours

- **GO**: All four success criteria are met (full validation of hypothesis).
- **NO-GO**: Any criterion fails (hypothesis not supported under realistic conditions).
- **PIVOT**: Partial success: e.g., Prediction 1 and 2 hold but Prediction 3 fails. Then investigate the role of particle size distribution more deeply (e.g., test different fractions of oversized particles).

### 6.1 Consensus

- L'hypothèse est physiquement bien fondée et s'appuie sur des concepts solides de mécanique granulaire (Γ, D/d, force chains), avec des prédictions falsifiables et un protocole de validation progressif bien structuré.
- Le problème industriel (colmatage dans les micro-réacteurs pour la chimie fine et les batteries) est réel et urgent, avec un potentiel d'impact fort si la solution est validée.
- La Phase 1 (simulation DEM) est peu coûteuse et à faible risque, et doit être lancée immédiatement pour tester la plausibilité du mécanisme avant d'engager des ressources expérimentales lourdes.

### 6.2 Points de desaccord

- Le mécanisme central (rupture des chaînes de force par vibration) est contesté par le domain_expert et le contrarian, qui soulignent que dans un slurry dense avec fluide visqueux (St < 10), les forces hydrodynamiques et de lubrification dominent, rendant l'analogie avec les milieux granulaires secs potentiellement invalide.
- Le contrarian identifie un angle mort critique : l'atténuation spatiale de la vibration dans une suspension dense (φ > 0.45) pourrait rendre le Γ local à la constriction 10 à 100 fois inférieur au Γ imposé, ce qui n'est pas mesuré ni contrôlé dans le protocole actuel.
- Le domain_expert et le contrarian estiment que l'hypothèse est prématurée sans preuve préliminaire (simulation DEM-CFD ou expérience simple), tandis que le methodologist et l'industrialist jugent le protocole suffisamment robuste pour justifier un test exploratoire.

### 6.3 Critical path

Le facteur le plus déterminant est la validation expérimentale ou numérique du mécanisme de fluidisation locale par vibration dans un slurry dense microfluidique, en particulier la mesure in situ de l'accélération locale (Γ effectif) à la constriction et la démonstration que la vibration brise effectivement les arches plutôt que de simplement modifier le profil de vitesse ou la pression de pore.

**Recommandation finale**: Le panel recommande la publication d'un brief de recherche pour lancer la Phase 1 (simulation DEM à 2k€) afin de tester rapidement la plausibilité du mécanisme. Cependant, les réserves du domain_expert et du contrarian sur le rôle du fluide interstitiel et l'atténuation de la vibration sont suffisamment sérieuses pour exiger que la Phase 1 inclue une validation préliminaire du Γ local (par exemple via une simulation DEM-CFD couplée). Si la Phase 1 échoue à montrer une réduction d'au moins 30% des blocages à Γ=3, l'hypothèse devra être abandonnée ou reformulée pour intégrer le couplage fluide-particule. En cas de succès, un brevet provisoire devra être déposé avant de passer aux Phases 2-3, idéalement avec un partenaire industriel.
